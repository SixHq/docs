Checkpoints are automatic snapshots of your workspace taken as Sixth works. They let you see exactly what changed at each step and roll back your files, the conversation, or both.

## How checkpoints work

Sixth saves a checkpoint after each change it makes (file edits, commands and so on). Checkpoints are kept in a separate shadow Git repository, so they never touch your own Git history, branches or staging area.

In the chat, each checkpoint is a thin line:

**Checkpoint** ────────── **Compare** · **Restore**

## Comparing

Click **Compare** to open a diff of everything that changed since that checkpoint.

## Restoring

Click **Restore** and choose:

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Restore files** | Puts your project's files back to the snapshot taken at this point. The conversation stays as it is. |
| **Restore task only** | Deletes the messages after this point. Your files aren't touched. |
| **Restore files & task** | Restores your files *and* deletes the messages after this point, for a clean restart from here. |

After **Restore files**, the checkpoint line is marked **(restored)**.

<Tip>Use **Compare** first to check exactly what a restore will undo.</Tip>

## Editing a message

Clicking one of your earlier messages lets you edit and resend it from that point. Press **Enter** (**Restore chat**) to roll back just the conversation, or **⌘/Ctrl + Enter** (**Restore all**) to also restore your files to that point. See [Editing messages](/features/editing-messages).

## When to use them

- **With auto-approve:** let Sixth move fast, knowing any step can be undone.
- **Trying alternatives:** restore files and ask for a different approach.
- **Exploring prompts:** restore the task only to try a different instruction while keeping the file changes.

## Turning checkpoints off

Checkpoints are on by default. Turn off **Enable Checkpoints** under **Settings → Features** if you don't want them; because they use Git under the hood, very large workspaces can be slow to snapshot.

## Deleting checkpoints

Checkpoints are stored with your task history in VS Code's global storage. Deleting a task from [history](/getting-started/task-management) deletes its checkpoints too; **Delete All History** removes all of them.
